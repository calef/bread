# Tangy Poolish Baguettes

Makes 3 baguettes. Yeast-leavened with a long, warm poolish that includes some whole wheat for extra tang.

## Ingredients

### Poolish (mix 18-20 hours before the final dough)
- Bread flour: 100 g
- Whole wheat flour: 50 g
- Water, room temperature: 150 g
- Instant yeast: 1/8 tsp

### Final dough
- Bread flour: 350 g
- Water, lukewarm: 200 g
- Salt: 10 g
- Instant yeast: 1/2 tsp
- All of the poolish

Overall hydration is 70% (350 g water to 500 g flour).

## Method

### Day 1: poolish
1. Stir the poolish ingredients with a spatula or fork (not a whisk, it is too thick) until no dry flour remains.
2. Cover and keep in a warm spot, around 75 °F, for 18-20 hours.
3. It is ready when very bubbly, domed, and just starting to sink in the middle. It should smell pleasantly sour.

### Day 2: dough
1. **Mix.** Add all of the poolish to the final-dough ingredients. Stir into a shaggy, sticky dough. No kneading. Do not forget the final-dough yeast.
2. **Rest.** Cover for 30 minutes.
3. **Stretch and fold.** With a wet hand, grab one side of the dough, stretch it up, and fold it over the center. Rotate the bowl and repeat 4 times. Do one more round 30 minutes later.
4. **Bulk ferment.** Cover and let rise until puffy, airy, and about 1.5x in size. Start checking at 1 hour. Typical range is 1.5-2 hours. Go by the dough, not the clock.
5. **Optional extra tang.** After the folds, refrigerate the covered dough for 24 hours, then continue from step 6 the next day.
6. **Divide and pre-shape.** Turn onto a lightly floured surface and cut into 3 equal pieces. Pat each into a rough rectangle and fold into a loose log. Keep the air in.
7. **Bench rest.** Cover with a towel for 15 minutes.
8. **Shape.** Pat a log into a rectangle. Fold the top third down over the middle and press the seam. Fold the bottom third up and press that seam. Roll gently from the center outward until about 35 cm (14 in) long, pressing a little harder at the tips to taper the ends.
9. **Proof.** Place seam-side down on the floured parchment you will bake on. Cover. Start the poke test at 30 minutes. It is ready when puffy and a fingertip dent springs back slowly and only partway. If the dent stays, it is overproofed.
10. **Preheat** (during the proof). Oven to 475 °F with a baking stone or inverted baking tray inside and an empty metal pan on a lower rack.
11. **Score and bake.** Slash each loaf 3-4 times lengthwise at a shallow angle in one confident motion with a sharp blade. Slide the parchment (with the loaves on it) onto the hot stone. Do not lift the dough itself. Pour 1 cup of hot water into the empty pan and close the door fast. Bake about 25 minutes, until deep golden and hollow-sounding.
12. **Cool** on a rack for at least 20 minutes before cutting.

## Notes
- Proofed dough deflates when lifted. Proof on the baking parchment and slide it onto the hot tray.
- If rise is poor across bakes, test the yeast: stir a pinch into warm water (about 110 °F) with a little sugar. It should foam within 10 minutes. Store opened yeast in the freezer.
- Longer poolish and a cold ferment both increase sourness.
