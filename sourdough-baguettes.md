# Sourdough Baguettes (with Starter Build)

Two parts: build a starter over 5-7 days, then bake baguettes with it. No commercial yeast.

## Part 1: Build the starter

Keep the jar around 75 °F. Cover loosely (a lid resting on top, or a coffee filter or cloth with a rubber band). Never seal it airtight, because the gas can crack the jar.

| Day | Action |
|-----|--------|
| 1 | Stir 50 g whole wheat flour and 50 g room-temperature water in a clean jar. |
| 2 | Leave it alone. It may bubble, then go quiet. That is normal. |
| 3-7 | Once a day: discard all but 50 g, then stir in 50 g bread flour and 50 g water. |

Tip: put a rubber band around the jar at the starter's level to track how much it rises.

The starter is ready when it doubles within 4-8 hours of feeding, is full of bubbles, and smells pleasantly tangy rather than like nail polish. If not ready at day 7, keep feeding daily.

## Part 2: Sourdough baguettes

Makes 3 baguettes.

### Ingredients
- Bread flour: 450 g
- Whole wheat flour: 50 g
- Water: 340 g
- Salt: 10 g
- Active starter (fed 4-8 hours earlier, at its peak): 100 g

### Method
1. **Mix.** Combine everything and stir into a shaggy, sticky dough. No kneading.
2. **Rest.** Cover for 30 minutes.
3. **Stretch and fold.** With a wet hand, grab one side of the dough, stretch it up, and fold it over the center. Rotate the bowl and repeat 4 times. Repeat the round every 30 minutes for the first 1.5-2 hours.
4. **Bulk ferment.** Cover and let rise until puffy, airy, and about 1.5x in size. With no added yeast this is slower, typically 4-6 hours at room temperature. Go by the dough, not the clock.
5. **Divide and pre-shape.** Turn onto a lightly floured surface and cut into 3 equal pieces. Pat each into a rough rectangle and fold into a loose log. Keep the air in.
6. **Bench rest.** Cover with a towel for 15-20 minutes.
7. **Shape.** Pat a log into a rectangle. Fold the top third down over the middle and press the seam. Fold the bottom third up and press that seam. Roll gently from the center outward until about 35 cm (14 in) long, pressing a little harder at the tips to taper the ends.
8. **Cold proof (most tang).** Place seam-side down on the floured parchment you will bake on. Cover and refrigerate 12-24 hours. Alternatively, proof at room temperature until puffy (roughly 1-2 hours) and bake the same day.
9. **Preheat.** Oven to 475 °F with a baking stone or inverted baking tray inside and an empty metal pan on a lower rack. Allow a full 30 minutes.
10. **Score and bake.** Slash each loaf 3-4 times lengthwise at a shallow angle in one confident motion with a sharp blade. Slide the parchment (with the loaves on it) onto the hot stone. Pour 1 cup of hot water into the empty pan and close the door fast. Bake about 25 minutes, until deep golden and hollow-sounding.
11. **Cool** on a rack for at least 20 minutes before cutting.

## Notes
- Poke test: a fingertip dent should spring back slowly and only partway. If the dent stays, it is overproofed.
- Proofed dough deflates when lifted. Proof on the baking parchment and slide it onto the hot tray.
- A longer cold proof (up to 24 hours) increases sourness. A shorter one tastes milder.
- Store the starter in the fridge between bakes and feed it weekly. Before baking, take it out and feed it 1-2 times at room temperature until active again.
